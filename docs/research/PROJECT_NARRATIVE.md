THE STORY BEHIND FORDAGO
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISITING THE CLIENT

  Before anything else was written or coded, the group first visited AFFORDA Gym
  in San Isidro, Nueva Ecija — the client and intended user of the system. The
  purpose of the visit was to understand how the gym currently operated and to
  present the initial concept of FordaGO to the gym owner.

  During the visit, the team explained the proposed system — a mobile-based gym
  management application that would digitize attendance tracking, member records,
  equipment management, scheduling, and inventory. The gym owner listened to the
  proposal and expressed full support for the project, giving the team the green
  light to proceed with the development.

  This visit was a crucial first step. It helped the team understand the real
  problems the gym was facing — manual logbooks for attendance, no organized
  system for tracking members, and no digital record of equipment usage. These
  problems became the foundation of everything the team built.

WEEK 1 — PLANNING AND PREPARATION

  With the client's approval secured, the team formally began the project. The
  first week was spent entirely on planning. No code was written yet — the focus
  was on laying the groundwork for everything that was to come.

  The group held discussions to decide on the technology stack, define the
  features of the system, and divide responsibilities among members. It was
  agreed that Delwin would lead the overall development and handle both the
  frontend and backend, with Ethan co-developing alongside him. Jaylee was
  assigned as the liaison officer to maintain communication with the client.
  Ryza took on the role of technical writer responsible for the thesis
  documentation. Carl was assigned to handle logistics, printing, and team
  support.

  The team also outlined the scope of the system during this period — what
  features would be included, what the database would look like, and how the
  mobile application would be structured. The system was designed to have two
  types of users: regular members and administrators, each with their own set
  of functions and access levels.

WEEK 2 — DEVELOPMENT BEGINS

  By the second week, the development work formally started. Delwin and Ethan
  began writing the code for FordaGO, working closely together and alternating
  tasks across both the frontend and backend of the system.

  On the frontend, they built the application using the Ionic Framework with
  Angular and TypeScript — a combination that allowed the app to run on both
  mobile devices and web browsers. On the backend, they set up a Node.js and
  Express REST API server connected to a MySQL database, which stored all the
  data for members, attendance, equipment, inventory, and more.

  While the development side was underway, the rest of the team was equally
  busy. Jaylee coordinated with AFFORDA Gym and continued gathering requirements
  and feedback from the client. Ryza began writing and compiling the thesis
  documentation, starting with Chapter 1 (Introduction) and Chapter 2 (Review
  of Related Literature and Studies). Carl handled all logistical needs —
  printing documents, procuring materials, and supporting the team in whatever
  way was needed.

WEEKENDS — CONSISTENT DEVELOPMENT WORK

  As the weeks went on, Delwin and Ethan made it a habit to dedicate their
  weekends to coding. Every weekend became a working session where new features
  were added, bugs were fixed, and the system gradually took shape. Progress
  was consistent — from the login and registration system, to the member
  dashboard, QR code equipment scanner, attendance tracking, workout scheduling,
  inventory management, and the admin panel.

  The team maintained open communication throughout, ensuring that what was
  being built on the technical side aligned with what was being documented and
  what the client expected.

CONSULTING THE ADVISER

  When the system was nearly complete, the group approached their capstone
  adviser for a consultation. The purpose was to present the working prototype
  and receive feedback on what was working well, what needed to be improved,
  and what should be removed or revised before the defense.

  The adviser's feedback was valuable. Certain aspects of the system were
  refined based on the recommendations received, and the documentation was
  updated accordingly. This consultation gave the team confidence that the
  system was heading in the right direction and was ready for formal evaluation.

CAPSTONE 1 DEFENSE — A SUCCESS

  The group presented FordaGO in their Capstone 1 defense, demonstrating the
  working prototype of the system to the panel. The presentation covered the
  background and objectives of the project, the technology used, the system
  features, and a live walkthrough of the application.

  The defense was a success. The panel acknowledged the completeness of the
  prototype and the clarity of the documentation. The experience was both
  rewarding and motivating for the entire group, validating the hard work and
  dedication that every member had contributed throughout the semester.

AFTER THE DEFENSE — CLIENT FEEDBACK

  Following the successful defense, the group returned to AFFORDA Gym to meet
  with the owner once more. This time, the purpose was to present the completed
  system prototype and let the client experience it firsthand.

  The gym owner was given a walkthrough of FordaGO's features — from member
  registration and attendance tracking to QR code scanning and report generation.
  The client's feedback was gathered to identify any final adjustments and to
  ensure the system truly addressed the needs of the gym. This post-defense
  consultation served as the bridge between the academic evaluation and the
  real-world application of the project.
