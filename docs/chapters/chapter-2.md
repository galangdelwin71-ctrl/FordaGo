CHAPTER II
RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

Research Design

This study will employ a Developmental Research Design in the development of FordaGO: Mobile-Based Gym Database Management System for AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch. Developmental research is appropriate for studies that involve the systematic design, development, testing, and evaluation of a product or system intended to address an identified problem. In this study, the developmental approach will be used to analyze existing gym management practices, design an appropriate mobile-based solution, develop the system, and evaluate its quality based on recognized software criteria.

The study will focus on the development of a mobile-based gym management system intended to address operational concerns at AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch, particularly in the areas of account and membership management, QR code-based attendance recording as the primary feature, equipment guidance access, session or workout schedule monitoring, notifications, and inventory or product management. Through this design, the researchers aim to produce a system that is both functional and relevant to the identified needs of the gym.

The development of the proposed system will follow the Agile Software Development Methodology, an iterative and incremental approach that emphasizes continuous improvement, adaptability to system requirements, and early delivery of working features (Pressman & Maxim, 2020). The system will be developed through a series of iterations focused on major modules such as user authentication, QR code-based attendance management, equipment information access, scheduling, notifications, and inventory-related functions. Feedback gathered from target users and evaluators will be used to refine the system during development. The final version of the system will be evaluated using selected ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics, namely functionality, usability, reliability, security, and performance efficiency.

System Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

This study will adopt the Agile System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) model. The Agile model is appropriate for this study because it supports iterative development, continuous refinement, and flexible adjustment of the system based on user feedback and observed requirements (Sommerville, 2019). The Agile SDLC for this study will be structured into the following phases:

Phase 1: Requirements Analysis

The researchers will gather system requirements through structured interviews and direct observation with the management, staff, and selected members of AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch. This phase will focus on identifying the existing operational problems and defining the functional requirements of the proposed system, including account management, QR code-based attendance, equipment information access, schedule monitoring, notifications, and inventory or product management.

Phase 2: System Design

Based on the identified requirements, the researchers will design the overall system architecture, database structure, user interface layouts, and process flow of the proposed system. This phase will include the design of the mobile application screens, QR code attendance process, equipment information interface, notification handling, session scheduling module, and relational database schema that supports the system.

Phase 3: Development

The system will be developed using an Ionic and Angular-based mobile frontend, a Node.js and Express.js backend, and a MySQL relational database. During this phase, the core functions of FordaGO will be implemented, including user authentication, member and admin access, QR code attendance recording, equipment guidance access, notification handling, session scheduling, and inventory or product management.

Phase 4: Testing

The developed system will undergo testing to identify and correct functional errors, interface issues, and performance concerns. Functional testing will be conducted to verify whether the system modules operate according to the specified requirements, while usability-focused observation and feedback will be used to determine whether the system is understandable and convenient for intended users.

Phase 5: Deployment and Evaluation

After development and testing, the system will be presented to the selected respondents for actual use, demonstration, and evaluation. Survey questionnaires will then be administered to gather respondent feedback using selected ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics. The gathered results will serve as the basis for the final analysis of the system.

Locale of the Study

This study was conducted at AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch, located in San Isidro. The gym was selected as the locale of the study because it provides the actual environment where the proposed system is intended to be used. The site is appropriate for the study because it involves routine gym operations such as member registration, attendance monitoring, equipment use, schedule handling, and inventory or product management, all of which are directly relevant to the proposed FordaGO system.

Respondents of the Study

The study employed purposive sampling, a non-probability sampling technique in which respondents are selected based on their relevance to the objectives of the study and their ability to provide meaningful evaluation of the proposed system (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). This method is appropriate because the evaluation of the system requires both technical assessment and actual user feedback.

A total of ten (10) respondents participated in the evaluation of the FordaGO system, categorized as follows:

IT Professionals or Technical Experts (5 respondents)

These respondents were individuals with knowledge or experience in software development, information systems, mobile application development, or database management. They were tasked with evaluating the technical quality of the system, particularly in terms of functionality, reliability, security, and performance efficiency.

End-Users (5 respondents)

These respondents were gym members or intended users from AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch. They were tasked with evaluating the system in terms of usability, accessibility, clarity of features, and overall user experience. Their feedback provided practical insight into how well the system meets the needs of actual users.

The combined evaluation of technical experts and end-users provides a more balanced and comprehensive assessment of the proposed system.

Research Instrument

The primary instrument used in this study was a structured survey questionnaire based on the ISO/IEC 25010 Systems and Software Quality Requirements and Evaluation (SQuaRE) model (ISO, 2011). This framework is widely used in evaluating software quality and is appropriate for assessing the effectiveness and acceptability of the proposed FordaGO system.

The questionnaire was designed to evaluate the system using the following quality characteristics:

Functional Suitability – Assesses whether the system correctly and completely performs the functions it is intended to provide, such as user account handling, attendance recording, equipment guidance access, scheduling, notifications, and inventory or product management.

Usability – Measures the ease with which users can understand, learn, and operate the system, including the clarity of its interface and overall user experience.

Reliability – Evaluates the ability of the system to operate consistently and accurately under normal conditions without unnecessary failures or interruptions.

Security – Assesses the capacity of the system to protect user information and restrict unauthorized access through authentication and role-based access features, in line with responsible handling of personal data.

Performance Efficiency – Measures the responsiveness and processing capability of the system while performing its required functions under expected usage conditions.

A four-point Likert scale was used in the questionnaire to encourage respondents to provide a definite assessment of each evaluation item. The exclusion of a neutral option helped obtain clearer positions from the respondents and supported more direct interpretation of the collected data.

Data Gathering Procedure

The researchers followed a structured data gathering procedure to ensure systematic and ethical conduct throughout the study. The procedure consisted of the following phases:

Phase 1: Preparation

The researchers prepared a formal request letter and secured permission from the management of AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch before conducting the study. Initial interviews and direct observations were also conducted to identify existing problems, understand operational workflows, and gather the requirements needed for the proposed system.

Phase 2: System Development

Using the gathered requirements, the researchers designed and developed the FordaGO system following the Agile Software Development Methodology. The system was built in iterative stages, with each stage focusing on selected modules and necessary improvements.

Phase 3: System Testing and Demonstration

After development, the researchers tested the system and conducted a demonstration for the selected respondents. The respondents were given time to explore the features and interact with the system before answering the evaluation instrument.

Phase 4: Data Collection

After the hands-on testing and demonstration, the structured survey questionnaires were distributed to the selected respondents. The researchers provided instructions and clarification as needed to ensure that the respondents fully understood the items in the instrument.

Phase 5: Data Analysis

The responses gathered from the survey questionnaire were tabulated, organized, and analyzed using an appropriate statistical treatment. The results were interpreted to determine the level of quality and acceptability of the proposed system.

Ethical Considerations

The researchers observed ethical standards throughout the conduct of the study in order to protect the rights, privacy, and welfare of all participants.

Permission and Consent. A formal request letter was submitted to the management of AFFORDA Gym – San Isidro Branch prior to conducting any research activity. The respondents were informed about the purpose, scope, and objectives of the study before participation. Participation was voluntary, and respondents were allowed to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty.

Confidentiality and Anonymity. The information gathered from respondents was treated with strict confidentiality. The identities of the respondents were not disclosed in the study, and the collected data were used solely for academic and research purposes.

Data Privacy and Security. All collected information was stored securely and was accessible only to the researchers. The FordaGO system also incorporates security-related features such as authentication and controlled user access to help protect stored information. The study adheres to the principles of responsible data handling under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

Statistical Treatment of Data

The data collected through the survey questionnaires were analyzed using the Weighted Mean (WM) as the primary statistical tool. The Weighted Mean is appropriate for Likert-scale responses because it summarizes the overall rating of each criterion based on the frequency and corresponding numerical weight of each response.

The formula used is:

WM = Σfx / N

Where:

WM = Weighted Mean
f = Frequency of each response
x = Numerical weight assigned to each response
N = Total number of respondents

The obtained weighted mean values were interpreted using the following 4-point Likert scale:

Scale    Range         Interpretation
4        3.26 - 4.00   Excellent / Strongly Agree
3        2.51 - 3.25   Very Good / Agree
2        1.76 - 2.50   Fair / Disagree
1        1.00 - 1.75   Poor / Strongly Disagree

This scale served as the basis for interpreting the responses and determining the overall acceptability and quality of the FordaGO system.
